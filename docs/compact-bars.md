## Changes applied after the third design review — not re-reviewed

1. Observed reset-due transitions are sticky for the particular snapshot and metric until a successful replacement fetch confirms new data. A backwards wall-clock change cannot resurrect the old metric in bars, dropdown or accessibility. The monitor owns snapshot-scoped latches; presentation-time observations cover delayed delivery and are handed back through local-only state processing, without credential or HTTP work. Give accepted snapshots an opaque process-local generation/identity so late observations cannot affect replacement snapshots or another binding. UI-side observations must also remain effective for that snapshot while the handoff is pending. New successful fetches may replace that snapshot; source changes/detach discard old-context state. Keep latches bounded to currently cached snapshots and clean them on replacement/detach/quit. Add a forward-past-reset then backwards-before-reset test, including delayed publication, with no successful fetch: old usage must remain reset-due/unranked. A subsequent successful replacement snapshot is tested separately.

2. The rendered dropdown signature includes balance and earned-reset-detail expiration states, not only quota reset state. Known expirations that affect current presentation join the existing coalesced one-shot deadline calculation, with the same crossed-deadline handling and future-only arming. At equality, show expired/expiry-unknown wording from the same presentation-time clock sample even if snapshot bytes and fetched-age minute bucket are unchanged. Do not manufacture a zero balance or decrement an authoritative/capped aggregate count from partial details: retain last-reported quantities with an explicit expired/cached qualifier. Observed expiration is snapshot-scoped and cannot become usable again merely because the wall clock moves backwards. Retain unknown expiry as unknown. Test balance and reset-credit expiration with unchanged snapshots, including exact equality and backwards clock change; no new per-second timer or network work.
